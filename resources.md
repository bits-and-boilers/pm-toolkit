# Program Manager Resources

A curated list of resources for **project, program, and technical program managers (TPMs)**, especially people moving from hands-on technical work or IT operations into program leadership.

**Cost key:** 🆓 free · 💲 free tier, trial, or audit option; paid for full use · 📕 paid

---

## Level 1: Foundations

*Core vocabulary, the project lifecycle, and the basics of scope, schedule, cost, and risk.*

- 💲 [IBM Project Manager Professional Certificate](https://www.coursera.org/professional-certificates/ibm-project-manager) (Coursera): beginner-friendly series that also prepares you for the CAPM exam.
- 🆓 [The Scrum Guide](https://scrumguides.org/scrum-guide.html): the official, short definition of Scrum from its creators. Read it once even if your shop isn't "agile," because everyone uses the vocabulary.
- 🆓 [Atlassian Team Playbook](https://www.atlassian.com/team-playbook/plays): free, step-by-step workshops ("Plays") for kickoffs, roles and responsibilities, retros, stakeholder communication, and more.

## Level 2: Program Management

*Running several related projects toward one outcome: governance, dependencies, stakeholders, and benefits.*

- 💲 [IBM Program Manager Professional Certificate](https://www.coursera.org/specializations/ibm-program-manager) (Coursera): moves from project to program thinking; aligned to PMI's PgMP.
- 📕 [PMI Certifications](https://www.pmi.org/Certifications): overview of PMI's credential ladder, including CAPM, PMP, PMI-ACP, and PgMP.
- 📕 [PMP Certification](https://www.pmi.org/certifications/project-management-pmp): the most widely requested PM credential in job postings. Check the experience requirements; years spent running real projects count.

## Level 3: Technical Program Management

*Leading programs where you have to understand the architecture, the systems, and the failure modes, not just the schedule.*

- 🆓 [Google SRE Books](https://sre.google/books/): three full books free online. The chapters on postmortems, on-call, release engineering, and service level objectives (SLOs) are essential reading for any TPM who owns production systems.
- 📕 [Technical Program Manager's Handbook](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/technical-program-managers/9781804613559/) (Packt/O'Reilly): a book-length look at the TPM role, the levels, and the career paths at large tech companies.

## From Operator to Program Leader

If you've spent years as the person who *keeps the systems running*, you already have more program management experience than your title suggests.

**What you already have**
- **Dependency mapping:** you know which systems break when another one changes.
- **Risk instincts:** you've lived through the outages, so you know where the real risks are.
- **Cross-team credibility:** engineers trust someone who has carried the pager.
- **Documentation:** runbooks, change records, and post-incident reviews are program artifacts.

**What to build on purpose**
- **Write for executives.** Lead with the decision you need and the business impact, then give the detail.
- **Separate status from health.** "On schedule" and "on track to deliver the outcome" aren't the same thing.
- **Delegate the fixing.** Your job moves from solving the problem to making sure the right person solves it.
- **Use a shared vocabulary.** Learn the PMI and agile terms so your experience translates on a resume and in an interview.
